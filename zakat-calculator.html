Zakat Calculator Pakistan

Add your savings and assets to check if Zakat is due and how much to pay.

How Zakat is calculated

Zakat is 2.5% of your net zakatable wealth if it stays above the Nisab for one lunar year. This calculator uses the silver Nisab of 52.5 tola (612.36 grams), which many scholars follow because it is the lower and safer threshold. Some scholars use the gold Nisab of 7.5 tola instead.

Zakat rulings can differ between schools of thought and individual situations. Please confirm details with a trusted scholar.

A simple 5-step way to prepare your Zakat

  1. Pick your Zakat date. Many people choose a fixed date each year, for example the 1st of Ramadan, so it never slips their mind.
  2. Write down everything. Cash, bank balances, gold, silver, business stock and money owed to you. Grab a notebook and list each item with its value.
  3. Subtract debts that are due soon. Short-term debts you must repay reduce your net wealth.
  4. Compare with the Nisab. If your net wealth is below it, Zakat is not due. If it is above it, 2.5% is due.
  5. Pay and keep a note. Record whom you paid and the amount, so next year is easier.

Tips to make Zakat easier

  • Use today's rate. Gold and silver prices change daily, so check a trusted local jeweller rate on the day you calculate.
  • Do not forget small savings. Committee (BC) money you have already received, wallet apps like Easypaisa or JazzCash balances and cash at home all count.
  • Plan ahead. If you are close to the Nisab, recheck a month before your Zakat date.
  • Ask when unsure. Questions about jewellery, shares, property or provident fund are best answered by a qualified scholar, because rulings can differ.

This calculator gives an estimate only and is not a religious ruling.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Nisab for Zakat?

The silver Nisab is 52.5 tola (612.36 grams) and the gold Nisab is 7.5 tola (87.48 grams). The value changes with market rates, so enter today's silver rate.

What percentage of Zakat is paid?

Zakat is 2.5% of your net zakatable wealth once it has stayed above the Nisab for a full lunar year.

Is personal-use jewellery included?

Gold and silver jewellery is generally counted as zakatable wealth, but opinions differ, so ask your scholar if you are unsure.
